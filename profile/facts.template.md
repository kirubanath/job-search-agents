## 5. Questions he asks on a call

What goes here: the questions you ask on a recruiter screen, the mandatory startup questions, and the bad answers that end a row. The list below is the one the jobs expect; change the wording if you like, but keep the numbers, because the jobs refer to them.

1. Is the role {{home_city}}-based, hybrid or remote, and how many office days?
2. What is the compensation band for this level?
3. How big is the team, and is there a separate product team and a manager who has been there more than a year?
4. What are the usual working hours, and when did the team last work a weekend?
5. What does the interview loop look like, and are AI tools allowed in the coding round?
6. (Startups, mandatory) What is the fixed cash component of the band for this role, excluding equity and variable?
7. (Startups, mandatory) When did the team last work a weekend, and why?
8. (Startups, mandatory) What are the on-call and after-hours expectations?
9. (Startups, mandatory) How many months of runway does the company have after the last round?
10. (Startups, mandatory) Who would I report to, and how long have they been at the company?
11. (Startups, mandatory) How did the team decide what to build last month?
12. (Startups, mandatory) What happened the last time a deadline slipped?

| Question | Bad answer | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | A fixed cash figure below the floor, or a refusal to give one before a second round | The row moves to Dropped, with the answer recorded |
| 9 | Runway under 12 months | The row moves to Dropped, with the answer recorded |
| 7 and 8 | No automatic bad answer | The answer is recorded in Notes. You decide whether to keep the row; a chat never drops a row for an hours reason |
| 10 to 12 | No automatic bad answer | You judge them yourself |

Equity never counts toward the floor. Before accepting an offer from a startup, speak to one engineer on the team who is not a founder.
